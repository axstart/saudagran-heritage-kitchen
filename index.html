The merchant house

From the 1920s Allahabad bazaar

Rasa-e-Lazzat carries a merchant-family kitchen. In 1920s Allahabad, the family traded whole spices — cardamom, clove, saffron — long before the recipes became heirlooms. Partition carried those stores to Pakistan; years in Saudi Arabia layered Arab warmth into South Asian heat. That lineage is now cooked in Kuala Lumpur, one sealed pot at a time.

Heritage engine

Over a century of heritage, told in three chapters

Allahabad → Pakistan → Saudi Arabia → Malaysia

Merchant-family spice trade in the Allahabad bazaars: whole cardamom, clove, and saffron bought by the sack, ground at home, and cooked into the first sealed pots the household still remembers.

Migration carried the masala tin to Pakistan, then to Saudi Arabia — where Arab hospitality and South Asian heat met. Dum cooking slowed further; pickles matured longer; the family palate became a fusion, never a compromise.

The kitchen now lives in Kuala Lumpur. Authentic Chicken Biryani, Shahi Tukray, Royal Shahi Dahi Baray, and Sophie's Mirch Ka Achar — for families across Greater Klang Valley who want the taste of home in every bite.
